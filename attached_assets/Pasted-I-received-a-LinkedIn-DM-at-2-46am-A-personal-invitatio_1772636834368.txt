I received a LinkedIn DM at 2:46am.

A personal invitation. Five hours away. The next day.

I said yes.

Then I checked who was speaking.

Three names I recognised.

Three people who had engaged with one of my posts days earlier.

We’d exchanged DMs. Brief, thoughtful conversations. Never once thinking we’d be in the same room anytime soon.

I messaged them. Told them I’d be there. We agreed to look out for each other.

First time I’d ever been to Manchester.

I walked into that room already with three familiar faces.

That changes the energy.

One of the people I connected with that afternoon was James.

We hadn’t spoken before. A complete stranger.

Within minutes we were deep into fatherhood, education, bilingual children, and what stoicism actually looks like in practice.

The kind of conversation that usually takes months of proximity, not minutes.

He recommended two thought leaders worth following. I’ve already started one of the podcasts.

Then something else happened.

“I wasn’t sure if it was you. But then I saw the earring.”

A few weeks ago, I’d shared a post on here about being reported at Clifford Chance for wearing a diamond earring. Not to my face. That’s how those things tend to go.

Someone in Manchester had seen it.

Across a room full of strangers, they made the connection and walked over.

Five hours from home.
Because of a post.
I hadn’t expected that.

Two days later, another personal invitation.
A legal lunch.

Two moments stood out.

The first. Someone who had commented on one of my posts: 
“Diamonds are FOREVER.”

As I walked toward my seat, I heard a gasp across the table.

“I know you from somewhere…”

A few curious looks from others.

“LinkedIn. I knew it was you!”

At first, I didn’t recognise her.

It was my first time attending, and I hadn’t expected to meet anyone I knew there.

But we did know each other.
Not from years of friendship.

From genuine engagement.

A comment that became a conversation.
A conversation that built familiarity.
Before we’d ever shared a room.

So there was no awkward beginning.
No cold handshake.

Just recognition and a warm embrace.

The second moment was different.

A complete stranger. No prior connection.
We simply sat next to each other.

She ordered the vegetarian option.

My daughter is two and we’re raising her vegetarian at the moment, so I’m always on the lookout for adventurous meal ideas she’ll enjoy eating. 

Naturally, we got talking.
Eventually we struck a deal.

I was tasked with monitoring her carb intake (three chips maximum! 😅) and in return, she promised to share some of her tasty vegetarian recipes she cooks with her own family. Ones I could try cooking with my daughter, Milán.

For just a moment, It felt less like business and more like being back at a family table.

I loved that.

She’s a partner at a premium boutique firm in London.

A perfect fit for what I’m building.

And we spent most of lunch talking about her son studying in Australia and debating whether my daughter would eat lentils!

That said, there’s something I should admit. 

Since joining LinkedIn a month ago, I’ve been deliberate.

Studying the craft.
Posting consistently.

Focusing on finding my voice.
And where I fit on the platform.

Some posts didn’t land.
Some ideas absolutely flopped.
But I didn’t obsess over the numbers.

I stayed consistent.
Stayed real.
And kept going.

And over time, something interesting started to happen.

Personal invitations into the right rooms. 
Each one traced back to something I had shared here.

But that wasn’t the most interesting part.

The people I mentioned in this story didn’t just connect and move on.

Each of us did something small that most people skip.

We replied. Not to feed the algorithm, but to continue the conversation.

We invested a little curiosity in someone we didn’t yet know.

At the time, none of us knew we’d end up in the same room.

There was no strategy behind it.
No transaction waiting at the end.

Just genuine engagement.

And when the room finally appears, the familiarity is already there.

That’s what consistent engagement actually does.

It doesn’t just grow numbers.
It builds familiarity before the room exists.

It turns a business lunch into something human.

Last week I crossed 500 followers.
I celebrated it.

No confetti. But still.

The younger version of me would have waited for a bigger number.

He would have missed the compound effect of small, consistent wins.

PS. If we’ve spoken on here, I’m probably already looking forward to being in the same room as you too one day.
