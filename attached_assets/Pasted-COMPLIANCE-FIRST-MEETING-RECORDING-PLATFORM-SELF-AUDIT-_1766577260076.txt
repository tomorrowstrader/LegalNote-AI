COMPLIANCE‑FIRST MEETING RECORDING PLATFORM – SELF‑AUDIT CHECKLIST
Instruction

Act as the firm’s Compliance Officer for Legal Practice (COLP) and Head of Risk.

Use this checklist to perform a thorough self‑audit of our meeting‑to‑attendance‑note platform (recording, transcription, diarisation, attendance notes and action extraction).

For each question:

Give a factual description of how the platform works today.

Mark status as Green (adequate), Amber (partial / unclear), or Red (not in place).

List 1–2 concrete remediation actions for any Amber or Red items (with suggested owner and timescale).

1. Scope, use‑cases and ownership
1.1 Functions and boundaries

What functions does the app perform today (e.g. recording, transcription, diarisation, attendance‑note drafting, action/obligation extraction)?

Does the app ever generate legal advice, legal conclusions, risk ratings, or case‑strategy suggestions (even implicitly)?

1.2 Intended use‑cases

In which matter types / practice areas is the app intended to be used (e.g. litigation, family, corporate, regulatory, private client)?

In which scenarios is the app not intended to be used (e.g. very sensitive or prohibited matter types)?

1.3 Internal ownership and accountability

Who inside the firm “owns” this tool (named partner, committee, or role)?

How is responsibility for oversight of this tool documented (e.g. AI register, tech governance paper, risk committee minutes)?

2. Legal and regulatory analysis (SRA, LSB, UK GDPR / DPA 2018)
2.1 SRA / LSB expectations

How does the app support compliance with SRA principles: confidentiality, supervision, competence, record‑keeping and accountability for outputs?

Have we produced a written note mapping this tool to SRA / LSB AI guidance and expectations?

2.2 Lawful basis and DPIA / AI risk assessment

What lawful bases have we identified for:

recording meetings

transcribing audio

generating and storing attendance notes

extracting and storing actions/obligations?

Have we completed a UK‑GDPR DPIA and/or AI‑specific risk assessment for this tool? If yes, where is it stored and when was it last reviewed?

2.3 Data subject rights and special‑category data

How does the system support data subject rights (access, rectification, restriction, erasure, objection) in relation to recordings and transcripts?

How have we assessed and documented handling of special‑category or criminal‑offence data that may arise in meetings?

3. Data handling, security and retention
3.1 Data flows and locations

Where are audio, transcripts, notes and audit logs stored (cloud provider, data centres, countries, sub‑processors)?

How are tenants, matters and users logically separated (multi‑tenant design, encryption keys, access controls)?

3.2 Retention policies and enforcement

What are our default retention periods for:

raw audio

raw transcripts

final attendance notes

audit logs?

Are these retention periods technically enforced in the system (automatic deletion / archiving), or only described in policy?

3.3 Security controls

What encryption is used for data in transit and at rest?

What authentication and authorisation mechanisms are in place (SSO, MFA options, role‑based access, least‑privilege)?

3.4 Sub‑processors and third‑party models

Which third‑party vendors, APIs or models process client data (speech‑to‑text, LLMs, storage, logging)?

Are there clear contractual restrictions on data use (no cross‑client training, no secondary use, no onward disclosure)?

4. Model behaviour and human oversight
4.1 AI components and error modes

Which parts of the workflow use AI / ML (e.g. speech recognition, diarisation, summarisation, action extraction)?

What are the known or expected error modes (mis‑hearing, mis‑labelling speakers, hallucinated points, missed actions)?

4.2 Human‑in‑the‑loop controls

At which points must a human (solicitor / supervised fee‑earner) review or approve outputs (transcripts, notes, action lists)?

Is human approval technically enforced (e.g. a “Mark as final attendance note” step) before notes or action lists become part of the matter record or are shared externally?

4.3 Quality assurance and supervision

Do we have any sampling or QA process (e.g. partner review of a percentage of AI‑assisted notes per month)?

How is supervision of junior users documented when they rely on the platform?

5. Consent, client communication and policy fit
5.1 On‑screen consent and notice

What consent / notice flows exist in the product (pre‑meeting emails, in‑meeting banners, verbal scripts, checkboxes)?

Do these flows explain recording and AI processing in clear, client‑friendly terms?

5.2 Alignment with firm documents

How do these flows align with the firm’s current engagement letters and privacy notice?

Have we updated those documents to refer to recording and AI‑assisted documentation where appropriate?

5.3 Red lines and exceptions

For which matter types or client circumstances (e.g. vulnerability, particularly sensitive issues) is recording discouraged or prohibited?

How are these exceptions communicated to users and enforced or flagged in the application?

6. Auditability and evidential value
6.1 Logged events

What events are logged (e.g. meeting created, recording started/stopped, consent recorded, audio deleted, transcript created, note edited/finalised, actions created, exports/shares, admin overrides)?

Which identifiers are stored for each event (timestamps, user IDs, matter IDs, IP / device info where appropriate)?

6.2 Reconstructing a matter

For a single meeting, can we reconstruct:

who initiated recording

which participants were informed and how

who accessed or edited recordings / transcripts / notes

who approved the final attendance note

what actions and deadlines were created?

How easily can these logs be exported for SRA audit, internal investigations, PI claims or complaints?

6.3 Authoritative record and disclosure

In case of discrepancy between audio, transcript and curated attendance note, which is treated as the authoritative record in practice?

How will recordings, transcripts, notes and logs be handled in disclosure, DSARs and regulatory requests, including privilege assessments?

7. Insurance and liability
7.1 PI insurer and broker engagement

Have we explained this tool to our PI broker/insurer (scope, data, controls, supervision)?

Have we documented their feedback, including any conditions, exclusions or recommendations?

7.2 Duty of fair presentation

Does our use of this platform need to be disclosed as a material circumstance for PI insurance?

If disclosed, how are we framing it (risk‑reduction control vs ungoverned AI)?

7.3 Vendor contract and liability

What does the vendor agreement say about:

data use and model training

security requirements and incident notification

liability caps and exclusions

indemnities (e.g. data protection / security breaches)?

Are these terms acceptable in light of our PI cover and risk appetite?

8. Operational readiness and change management
8.1 Integrations and resilience

Which integrations are live (PMS, DMS, Outlook/Google calendars, SSO, task systems)?

What happens if the platform or an integration is unavailable during a live matter (fallback processes, manual workaround)?

8.2 Implementation and vendor risk

How is vendor risk assessed (financial health, roadmap, independent assurance reports, penetration tests)?

What is our exit plan if we need to stop using the tool (data export formats, data deletion, transition to alternatives)?

8.3 Policies, training and monitoring

What internal policies now govern use of this tool (AI usage policy, recording policy, information security policies)?

What training have we provided to partners, fee‑earners and support staff on correct use and limitations?

How will we monitor actual usage (e.g. adoption stats, exceptions, incident reports) and periodically re‑assess the risk profile?

9. Remediation and action plan
For every Amber or Red answer above, list:

Issue / gap (short description)

Related section/question

Proposed remediation action

Owner (role / team)

Target timescale (e.g. 1–3 months, 3–6 months)

Use this consolidated list as your implementation roadmap before or alongside wider rollout.
