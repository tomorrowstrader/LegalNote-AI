POST 5: The Fear of Being Copied (SLAY + Relatable Founder Story)
Hook: I almost stayed quiet.
Rehook: That would have been my biggest mistake.

For months, I didn't talk about what I was building.
Not because I was embarrassed.
Because I was scared.
"What if someone copies this?" "What if a bigger player sees it and builds their own?" "What if I'm giving away the playbook?"
So I stayed quiet. Built in silence. Told no one.
Then I onboarded my first client.
They said something that changed everything:
"We've been looking for this for years. Why haven't we heard of you?"
That's when it hit me:
The risk isn't someone copying you.
The risk is the right people never finding you.
Every day you stay quiet, a decision-maker who needs your solution is settling for something worse - or building a workaround that will fail them later.
Your competitors aren't reading your LinkedIn posts looking for ideas.
Your future clients are.
Speak to them.
The people who can copy you won't. The people who need you can't find you if you're silent.
PS - What are you not sharing because you're afraid someone might steal it?

POST 6: Sophisticated Buyers (PAS + Authority Positioning)
Hook: Cheap buyers buy on price.
Rehook: Sophisticated buyers buy on trust.

I learned this the hard way.
Early on, I pitched LegalNote to anyone who'd listen.
Some wanted the cheapest option. Some wanted the fastest demo. Some wanted features we didn't have.
I chased all of them.
Closed none of them.
Then I changed my approach.
I stopped selling features.
Started educating on risk:
"Here's what happens when a client disputes your advice." "Here's what the SRA expects from your documentation." "Here's what your PI insurer will ask for."
The cheap buyers disappeared.
The sophisticated buyers leaned in.
They didn't ask "how much does it cost?"
They asked "how does the audit trail work?"
That's when I realised:
Sophisticated buyers buy from sophisticated authorities.
If you want premium clients, stop competing on price.
Start competing on expertise.
Educate the market. Demonstrate depth. Show your methodology.
The right clients will self-select.
PS - Are you attracting buyers who question your price, or your process?

POST 9: Why Generic Tools Fail Solicitors (PAS + Educational)
Hook: Your note-taking app isn't built for you.
Rehook: And it's creating liability.

I see it constantly:
Solicitors using Otter.ai. Or Microsoft Dictate. Or Apple Voice Memos.
"It's fine, it records and transcribes."
But here's what they're missing:
Consumer tools don't understand legal requirements.
No consent documentation → GDPR breach risk. No audit trail → Evidence inadmissible. Cloud storage location unknown → Potential data sovereignty issues. No client-matter linking → Discovery nightmare.
These aren't edge cases.
They're the difference between defensible practice and professional negligence.
When a client says "I never agreed to be recorded" -
Can you prove otherwise?
When opposing counsel challenges your attendance note -
Can you demonstrate it hasn't been altered?
When the SRA asks for your documentation process -
Can you show a systematic approach?
The tools most solicitors use today were built for:
* Journalists interviewing sources
* Students recording lectures
* Salespeople logging calls
Not for professionals whose documentation might be examined under forensic scrutiny.
The solution isn't "be more careful."
It's infrastructure designed for your actual risk environment.
PS - Does your current documentation tool know what the SRA expects?

Hook: The biggest legal tech companies forgot about you.
Rehook: That's their mistake, not yours.

Watch any legal tech announcement.
Harvey. Legora. The venture-backed giants.
Notice where they're focused:
AmLaw 100 firms. $1,000/hour partners. US market TAM.
Makes sense for them.
Bigger firms = bigger contracts = bigger returns for investors.
But here's what gets ignored:
The UK boutique firm.
The 3-partner practice in Manchester. The specialist employment firm in Leeds. The sole practitioner in Bristol.
These firms have the same compliance obligations as Magic Circle firms.
The same SRA requirements. The same GDPR exposure. The same PI claim risk.
But they don't have the same budget.
So they're stuck choosing between:
* Enterprise software they can't afford
* Consumer tools that don't fit
* Manual processes that create risk
This is the gap.
Not another AI that drafts contracts for BigLaw.
Infrastructure that makes boutique firms audit-ready at a price that makes sense.
UK-built. UK-hosted. UK-focused.
Because sometimes the best market isn't the biggest one.
It's the most underserved one.
