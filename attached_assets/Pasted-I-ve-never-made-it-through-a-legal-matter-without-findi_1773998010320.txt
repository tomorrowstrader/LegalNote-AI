I’ve never made it through a legal matter without finding an error in the documentation.

Not one.

Ten years. Multiple solicitors. Multiple firms.

Music publishing contracts.
Tenancy disputes.
Medical negligence.
And other matters I can’t detail here due to their sensitivity.

Sometimes I caught it immediately.
Sometimes months later.

Once, years after the matter had closed, I found it in an old file.

I was building LegalNote at the time, going back through my own documentation to understand the problem I was trying to solve.

The solicitor had told me to plead guilty.
The attendance note said not guilty.

I did what I was told.
The record said otherwise.

That moment stayed with me.

Because the advice was right.
But the evidence of it wasn’t.

For a long time, I didn’t understand how this keeps happening.

These were good solicitors. Careful people.
People I trusted.

Then I thought back to what I had seen from the inside.

Time spent within Clifford Chance.
Charles Russell Speechlys.

Sitting alongside fee earners.
Watching the gap between what was said in the room, and what made it into the file.

And later, speaking directly to solicitors while building LegalNote.

I began to understand something most clients never see.

You are managing someone’s most stressful day, while carrying twenty others at the same time.

You leave a meeting where someone has just broken down about losing their home.
Then go straight into a call about an exchange collapsing on another matter.

The attendance note from that first meeting gets written later.
From memory.
Between everything else that couldn’t wait.

Not because you don’t care.

Because the structure of the work doesn’t allow you to hold everything, exactly as it happened, at the moment it needs to be recorded.

What I experienced over ten years wasn’t negligence.

It was something much more human.

A profession that depends on memory, operating under constant pressure.

And memory, under pressure, is not as reliable as we like to believe.

That’s where the gap appears.

And once something is challenged, the file becomes the only version of events that exists.

I stopped being frustrated when I understood that.
