15 Hidden Compliance Traps in Legal Meeting Documentation
A self-assessment checklist for UK solicitors — with regulatory references

Prepared for {{RECIPIENT_FULL_NAME}}

Most firms don't fail audits because of bad intent. They fail because common industry practices have become normalised — even when they create regulatory exposure. This checklist surfaces the traps that hide in plain sight.

Recording & Consent Traps

1
No Recorded Consent Before Recording Begins
Starting audio/video capture before obtaining explicit, documented consent. Verbal "okay" without timestamp is insufficient.
GDPR Art. 6(1)(a), ICO Guidance on Consent
High Risk
2
Using Zoom/Teams Without Understanding Data Flows
Client data processed through US servers without adequate safeguards. Most "default" settings involve third-country transfers requiring SCCs.
GDPR Art. 44-49, Schrems II Ruling, ICO International Transfer Guidance
High Risk
3
No Audio Retention Policy
Keeping recordings indefinitely or having no documented deletion schedule. Audio becomes a disclosure liability in unrelated matters.
GDPR Art. 5(1)(e) Storage Limitation, SRA Code of Conduct for Firms 4.2
High Risk

Attendance Note Traps

4
Notes Created Hours/Days After Meeting
Attendance notes drafted from memory rather than contemporaneously. Courts and insurers treat delayed records as less reliable evidence.
SRA Code of Conduct for Solicitors 7.2-7.5 (Service and Competence), PI Insurance Best Practice
High Risk
5
No Timestamp Proving When Note Was Created
Document creation time not independently verifiable. Metadata can be manipulated; cryptographic timestamps cannot.
Civil Procedure Rules Practice Direction, Civil Evidence Act 1995
Medium Risk
6
Unclear Attribution of Who Said What
Notes paraphrase discussions without identifying speakers. In disputes, "we discussed" becomes contested territory.
SRA Code of Conduct for Solicitors 7.1 (Competence), PI Defence Requirements
Medium Risk
7
Edits Made Without Version History
Corrections or additions overwrite original content. No audit trail of what changed, when, and by whom.
SRA Code of Conduct for Firms 4.2 (Record Keeping), GDPR Art. 5(1)(f) Integrity
Medium Risk

Data Protection Traps

8
Third-Country Transfers via "Free" Transcription Tools
Using Otter.ai, Rev, or similar services that process data outside UK/EU. Client confidential information crosses borders without DPIA.
GDPR Art. 44-49, SCCs, UK Adequacy Regulations
High Risk
9
No Data Processing Agreement with AI/Transcription Provider
Using AI tools without signed DPA. You remain controller; processor obligations must be contractually bound.
GDPR Art. 28, ICO Controller-Processor Guidance
High Risk
10
Unable to Respond to DSAR Within 30 Days
Meeting records scattered across devices, cloud storage, email. No centralised system to locate all personal data.
GDPR Art. 15, ICO DSAR Guidance
Medium Risk

Audit & Defensibility Traps

11
No Tamper-Evident Audit Trail
Document access, edits, and exports not logged. In disputes, you cannot prove chain of custody or integrity.
SRA Code of Conduct for Firms 4.2, Civil Evidence Act 1995
Medium Risk
12
Client Documents Stored on Personal Devices
Meeting notes on solicitor's personal phone/laptop. Data leaves the firm when they do; no centralised control.
GDPR Art. 32 Security, SRA Code of Conduct for Firms 4.2
Medium Risk
13
No Documented Workflow for Capturing Action Items
Follow-ups and deadlines mentioned but not systematically recorded. Missed actions become negligence claims.
SRA Code of Conduct for Solicitors 7.1, Firms 4.2, Professional Negligence Standards
Medium Risk
14
Sharing Client Documents Without Redaction Capability
Sending full transcripts when only excerpts needed. Over-disclosure creates privilege waiver and data breach risks.
GDPR Art. 5(1)(c) Data Minimisation, Legal Privilege Rules
Medium Risk
15
No PI-Ready Evidence Package
When a claim arises, scrambling to reconstruct what happened. Evidence should be pre-organised, not post-hoc assembled.
PI Insurance Requirements, SRA Indemnity Rules
High Risk
