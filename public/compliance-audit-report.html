High-Priority Findings

1. Third-Country Data Transfers via Transcription Tools
High Risk
Current State: The firm uses consumer-grade transcription services that process client meeting audio through US-based servers without appropriate transfer mechanisms.

Risk: Post-Schrems II, transfers to the US require either Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) with supplementary measures or explicit client consent. Consumer tools rarely provide adequate DPAs or SCCs.

Impact: Potential ICO enforcement action, client complaints, and reputational damage. GDPR fines can reach 4% of annual turnover or £17.5m.
GDPR Articles 44-49, Schrems II (Case C-311/18), ICO International Transfer Guidance
2. Non-Contemporaneous Attendance Notes
High Risk
Current State: Attendance notes are typically drafted from memory, hours or days after client meetings conclude.

Risk: In negligence claims and regulatory investigations, delayed records are treated as less reliable evidence. The gap between conversation and written record is where disputes arise.

Impact: Weakened defence in PI claims, difficulty demonstrating advice given, increased insurance exposure.
SRA Code of Conduct Para 2.4, 3.2; Professional Negligence Best Practice; Civil Evidence Considerations
3. No Tamper-Evident Audit Trail
High Risk
Current State: Document access, edits, and exports are not logged in a tamper-evident manner. Standard file metadata can be manipulated.

Risk: In disputes, you cannot prove chain of custody or that records were not altered after the fact. This undermines evidential value.

Impact: Contested evidence in litigation, SRA concerns about record integrity, PI claim vulnerability.
SRA Code of Conduct for Firms 4.2, Civil Procedure Rules, Civil Evidence Act 1995

Prioritised Recommendations

1
Implement EU/UK-Only Processing for Client Data
Migrate transcription and meeting documentation to services with guaranteed UK/EU data residency and appropriate DPAs. Timeline: Immediate (within 30 days).
2
Establish Consent-First Recording Protocol
Implement documented, timestamped consent capture before any meeting recording begins. Consent should be part of the audio file itself. Timeline: Within 60 days.
3
Adopt Real-Time Documentation Workflow
Replace memory-based note-taking with contemporaneous capture that creates records during or immediately after meetings. Timeline: Within 90 days.
4
Implement Audio Retention Policy
Establish documented policy for audio deletion (recommend 7 days post-transcription). Transcripts retained; audio eliminated to reduce disclosure liability. Timeline: Within 60 days.
5
Deploy Tamper-Evident Audit Logging
Implement cryptographically signed audit trails for all meeting documentation access, edits, and exports. Timeline: Within 90 days.
