# LinkedIn Posts — Growth & Meeting-to-Matter Campaign

> **Proven formula:** Jazz's Clifford Chance diamond earring post (~22k impressions, 477+ reactions, 162 comments).
> **ICP:** UK firms £750k–£1.5m, 4–8 fee earners, growing team.
> **Link (soft only):** legalnote.ai/start?src=linkedin — put in first comment, not the post body.
> **Visual:** Use a strong personal photo (lifestyle-professional, you in frame). Not product screenshots. Not carousels for story posts.

---

## What the diamond earring post taught us

| Element | What you did | Why it worked with ICP |
|---------|--------------|------------------------|
| **Scroll-stopper first line** | Named Clifford Chance + diamond earring | Prestige + specificity = instant curiosity |
| **Scene, not summary** | First day → next morning → manager aside | Readers watch a film, they don't read a pitch |
| **Short paragraphs** | 1–3 lines each | Mobile-native. Easy to skim. Easy to feel |
| **Pattern, not rant** | Music school parallel | Turns personal pain into a *pattern* others recognise |
| **Worldview, not product** | "Documentation speaks louder than perception" | Soft bridge to LegalNote without selling |
| **PS that invites identity** | "Have you ever felt like you didn't belong…" | People comment with *their* story. Algorithm loves this |
| **Face in the frame** | Full-body professional photo | Trust + shareability. Your ICP engages with *you* |
| **Almost no CTA** | No "book a demo" | Soft sell = more comments. Hard sell = lurkers |

**Core rule going forward:** Lead with a story that makes managing partners feel seen. Let LegalNote be the worldview that emerges — not the headline.

---

## The formula to repeat

```
1. First line = specific + surprising + named world (firm / room / moment)
2. 4–8 short beats of scene
3. Name the pattern (not the villain)
4. Parallel or realisation (optional but powerful)
5. Soft pivot: "So I built / That's why / I chose…"
6. One-line worldview that maps to LegalNote
7. PS — identity question (belonging, responsibility, fear) — not a product question
8. Strong personal photo
9. Product link ONLY in first comment (or not at all)
```

**Comment engagement loop:** Reply to every comment in the first 2 hours with a real line, not "thanks!". Ask one follow-up. This is how 162 comments happens — not the post alone.

## Post 1: The room you're responsible for (Growth / oversight)

**Angle:** Same emotional register as the earring post — responsibility without visibility.
**Photo:** You, professional, thoughtful. Corridor / office energy.

**Draft:**

I can't be in every client meeting.

Neither can most managing partners I speak to.

They're growing. Hiring. Training. Winning work.

And still responsible for every file.

That's the quiet pressure nobody posts about.

Not "will the SRA call." Something sharper:

What was said in the room when I wasn't there?

Was it documented to our standard — or reconstructed at 6pm from memory?

Does the new starter's note look like mine would have?

I spent years in Magic Circle environments where perception often outran the work.

So I built the opposite.

A company where documentation speaks louder than perception. Where the meeting becomes the proof — on the matter, while it still matters.

Not so firms can survive audits.
So they can grow without the quality slipping.

PS — When you're not in the room, how do you know the file still reflects your standard?

## Post 2: The six-month conversation that came too late (Shak story, anonymised)

**Angle:** Scene storytelling. Commercial consequence. Soft Meeting-to-Matter close.
**Photo:** You, or a quiet office / boardroom still (ceiling light energy worked before).

**Draft:**

A partner told me about a deal that ran six months.

Three people touched it.

At the final check, he found a conversation that should have happened with the client — hadn't.

So he called them. At the most sensitive point. Explained it properly.

They said they hadn't understood. Nobody had told them.

They pulled out.

Commission gone. Relationship gone. Months of work — gone.

The problem wasn't malice.

Nobody could see what happened in the meetings until the end.

If that gap had been visible in week two, the conversation would have happened while trust was still intact.

I hear versions of this from growing firms constantly.

Not "we need more AI."

"I need to see what's happening in meetings I'm not in — before it's too late."

That's why LegalNote exists.

Meeting-to-Matter. Every client conference captured. Documented. On the file.

So visibility arrives early. Not at the cliff edge.

PS — Have you ever found out something critical about a matter — too late to save it gracefully?

## Post 3: Documentation as proof (Founder origin bridge)

**Angle:** Closest to your winning post. Personal. Belonging. Soft product worldview.
**Photo:** Same aesthetic as the viral one — you, intentional, full presence.

**Draft:**

Someone once decided, behind my back, that something about how I presented myself wasn't acceptable.

No policy. No conversation. Just a quiet judgment.

I couldn't control how people judged me.

But I could make myself impossible to question.

So I became sharper. More intentional. The kind of person who walked into a room and left no gap for scrutiny.

Not because I owed them that.
Because I owed it to myself.

Years later, building LegalNote, I realised I'd been circling the same idea the whole time:

The work should be the proof.

Not perception. Not politics. Not who was in the corridor when the decision was made.

For solicitors, that means something concrete.

The client meeting. Captured. Documented. On the matter.

So when someone asks "was this explained?" — the answer isn't a reconstruction.
It's on the file.

A company where documentation speaks louder than perception.

That's still the north star.

PS — Have you ever felt like you were delivering — and still being judged on something that had nothing to do with the work?

## What NOT to do (kills engagement)

- Lead with SRA / compliance / "defensible by design" in the first 3 lines
- Feature lists, pricing, "5 founding firm slots" in story posts (save for soft CTAs later)
- Product screenshots as the hero image on personal-story posts
- Asking "Would you buy X?" — ask identity questions instead
- Over-explaining LegalNote before the emotional beat lands
